Hornet does not inherit Hallownest. She inherits its debts. *Hollow Knight: Silksong* drops you in **Pharloom**, a pilgrimage kingdom that treats curiosity as a tax, and Team Cherry still refuse to hold your hand while they empty your wallet of rosaries.

This is a scored review, not a recap of someone else's. I played on Switch and PC, died in the same stupid places on both, and came away convinced the seven-year wait produced a real sequel instead of a skin.

## What Silksong actually is

You play Hornet: faster, more aerial, more combative than the Knight. Silk is a resource you spend and a lifeline you earn. The map is a vertical sermon — citadels, moss, bells, cages — and the comedy of bugs remains intact even when the combat is not being funny at all.

For the "should I tap buy on eShop" version, see [Is Silksong worth buying in Australia?](/silksong-worth-buying-australia/).

## Combat and the early wall

The opening hours are the review's only real warning label. Hornet's kit is expressive — dive, bind, crest loadouts later — but Pharloom's first biomes assume you already speak Hollow Knight. New players will bounce. Veterans will grin, then bounce anyway.

Bosses are the thesis. Telegraphs are readable; patterns still ask for humility. When a fight finally clicks, the room design around it usually deserved the attempt count.

I never felt a boss cheat. I often felt a bench placement make a political argument.

## Exploration, economy, benches

Silksong's best trick is making traversal feel like an argument you are winning. Shortcuts are earned. The rosary economy is tight enough that a careless run back actually hurts, which is the point. Side wishes give Pharloom a civic texture Hallownest mostly implied.

The map does not cheat. It judges. That is Team Cherry being Team Cherry, and it is why the high score is not a 10 — the early spike will lock some players out of a game that later becomes generous.

## How it compares to Hollow Knight

Hallownest was melancholy space. Pharloom is industry and faith with sharper teeth. The sequel is denser: more systems on screen, more quest texture, less empty corridor. It is also less immediately kind. That is a design choice, not a bug, and it will split the audience the original united.

For the original's place in the catalogue, start at the [Hollow Knight hub](/games/hollow-knight/). For Hornet's game, stay on the [Silksong hub](/games/hollow-knight-silksong/).

## Consensus, credited

I do not need other outlets to form a score. I do use them as a temperature check.

According to [IGN's Tom Marks](https://www.ign.com/articles/hollow-knight-silksong-review), Silksong is "a bigger, bolder, and even more impressive adventure than the original," scored **9/10**. That lines up with the shape of my playthrough: more game, more demand, more payoff. Marks is kinder on the early spike than some Australian threads I have read; I am slightly less forgiving there, and still land in the same buy-it band.

## Australia: price, platforms, should you?

On AU storefronts this remains an indie-priced download, not a $79–$129 first-party event. That matters. A vicious Metroidvania at that price is an easier recommend than another $120 live-service.

Play on **Switch** if you want handheld death loops on the tram. Play on **PC** if you want the cleaner frame pacing I saw in longer sessions. Either way, buy it if Hallownest still lives in your thumbs. Skip it if you bounced off the original's runbacks and never made peace with them — Silksong will not convert you.

**Score: 9.2 / 10.**
