If *Overcooked* was a dinner party argument, *Overcooked 2* is the restaurant opening you should not have booked. Ghost Town Games kept the four-player kitchen chaos and added portals, rideable birds, moving counters, and a campaign that understands escalation. You will shout. You will plate the wrong order. You will still queue the next level.

## Mise en place, then disaster

The sequel's kitchens are more theatrical. Rivers cut the floor in half. Conveyor belts steal your chopped onion. A well-run kitchen in this game is not about being the best cook — it is about being the least chaotic communicator.

On Switch, local multiplayer is the platonic ideal: four Joy-Con, one TV, zero dignity. PlayStation and Xbox handle online more comfortably, which matters if your regular crew is split between states. PC remains the cleanest input experience if you have enough controllers in one room.

## The secret ingredient

Level design. The best stages teach a new rule and then immediately abuse it. You learn to throw ingredients, then the game asks you to throw them across a moving platform during a dinner rush. It is cruel in a way that feels authored rather than random.

The recipe list is broader, the scoring is tighter, and the co-op fantasy is still unmatched: four people becoming one organism that can somehow burn soup.

## Overdone bits

Solo play is a thinner dish. The AI chefs are competent enough to finish a campaign, but they will never recreate the human disaster that makes the game special. A few late levels also spike into coordination checks that feel like exams.

## Verdict

Still the kitchen game to beat. **8.8/10** — buy it for the group, keep it for the stories. If your friendship survives the sushi boat, it can survive anything.
