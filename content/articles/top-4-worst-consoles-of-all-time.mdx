Worst does not mean "sold poorly" and it does not mean "I hated the mascot." Worst means the hardware, the business, or the software strategy actively made games worse. Here are four machines that earned the museum of mistakes.

## 4. Philips CD-i

A compact disc player that wandered into the living room and tried to be a console. Nintendo's reluctant licensing produced *Hotel Mario* and a *Zelda* that still circulates as a warning. The CD-i is fascinating as a dead end: extra media, extra menus, extra ways to miss the point of a control pad.

## 3. Jaguar

Atari's 64-bit boast could not hide a developer kit that felt like a dare. A handful of interesting games does not rescue a machine that made third parties flee. The Jaguar is the sound of a company remembering it used to be first.

## 2. Virtual Boy

A headset that was a table. A colour palette that was a medical symptom. Nintendo's name is on it, which is the only reason we still talk about it with affection. As a product, it is a migraine with a cartridge slot.

## 1. Atari Jaguar CD / the add-on era's worst instincts

Honourable mention to every add-on that split a user base (32X, we see you). We are giving the crown to the instinct itself: ship an incomplete machine, then sell the rest later. Libraries die that way. Players remember.

The lesson is not "old hardware bad." The lesson is that a console is a promise to developers. Break that promise and the museum will keep your plastic.
