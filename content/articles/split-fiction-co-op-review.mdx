Josef Fares keeps making games that treat a second player as a mechanic, not a checkbox. *Split Fiction* is the most elastic version of that idea yet: sci-fi and fantasy halves, set-pieces that change genre mid-sentence, and a campaign that wants you to talk the entire time.

## Designed for interruption

The best levels are the ones that force asymmetric jobs. One player aims; the other navigates. One player is a tank; the other is a problem. When it clicks, you get the *It Takes Two* high without repeating the marriage counselling. When it does not click, you get a valuable data point about your friendship.

Set-piece variety is the flex and the risk. A weaker chapter is still a new toy. A stronger chapter is the reason you will recommend the game at dinner.

## The seams

Some transitions are louder than they need to be. Solo-with-AI is not the point and should not be sold as one. Online play is stable enough in our sessions; same-couch is still the intended cut.

## Australia: the other stick

This is a two-weekend game if you stop arguing. It is a four-weekend game if you do not. On PSN AU and Steam AU it goes on sale often enough that full-price is a "we have a friend flying in on Friday" decision, not a lifestyle.

Plus Extra has hosted Hazelight before. If Split Fiction is in the Extra catalogue the week you read this, that is the correct price. If it is not, the [Split Fiction hub](/games/split-fiction/) and our [Best PS5 games 2026](/best-ps5-games-2026/) list are the context: this is still one of the best reasons to keep a second DualSense charged.

## Verdict

**8.7/10**. If you have a co-op person, this is a weekend. If you do not, borrow one. Hazelight remains the studio that remembers multiplayer can be authored.
