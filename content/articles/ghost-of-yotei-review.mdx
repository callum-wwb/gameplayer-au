The first time I let the Guiding Wind take me toward Mount Yōtei I was standing on a Melbourne balcony with the DualSense speaker doing that cheap theatre trick it does — shamisen in the haptics, not the speaker — and I thought: Sucker Punch still know how to sell a horizon. *Ghost of Yotei* is not a new idea. It is a colder one. That is enough, until the hunt starts rhyming.

This is a scored GamePlayer review from a PS5 playthrough (Performance on the base unit, Ray Tracing Pro on a Pro I borrowed for a week). It is not a recap of someone else's weekend. For the collection point, stay on the [Ghost of Yotei hub](/games/ghost-of-yotei/). For the calendar and till argument, we filed a separate [Australian release note](/ghost-of-yotei-release-date-australia/).

## What Yotei actually is

You are **Atsu**, a mercenary who survived a massacre at sixteen and came back to **Ezo** — the island we now call Hokkaido — to cross six names off a sash. The Yotei Six work for Lord Saito. The people in the taverns have already decided Atsu is an *onryō*, a grudge that refused to die. She does not argue with the branding.

The game is a standalone follow-up to *Ghost of Tsushima*, set roughly three centuries later. You do not need Jin's story. You will feel the grammar immediately: wind as a compass, standoffs as punctuation, shrines as skill points, a photo mode mapped where a lesser game would have put a map ping.

Sucker Punch moved the camera north and swapped the honour lecture for a revenge that does not pretend it is noble. Erika Ishii plays Atsu like someone who has already spent the apology. That is the review, before the weapons.

## Combat: the reason the DualSense stays warm

Tsushima taught you stances. Yotei hands you a **kit**. Katana for other swords, yari for the dual-wield pests, kusarigama for shields, odachi for the people who look like they eat horses. The lock-and-key is the same joke with louder props. I still swapped because each weapon has a weight the DualSense bothers to finish.

The kusarigama is the new sentence. Yank a sentry off a ridge. Crack a pavise. Clear a courtyard without looking like a stance menu. Gold attacks will disarm you if you get greedy; answering with a charged heavy and throwing the dropped blade at the next idiot is the closest the series has come to feeling scrappy instead of ceremonial.

Stealth is still tall grass and backs turned. The AI will forgive a murder it watched. I stopped pretending I was a ghost after the third camp and started treating stealth as a prologue to the fun part. Sucker Punch seems to agree. If you get caught, the game shrugs and lets you fight — which is, inconveniently, the best thing it does.

Duels are the punctuation. Perfect parries still make the room go quiet. Standoffs still make you sit forward. Onryō's Howl — the Ghost Stance cousin — is louder, uglier, and more honest about what Atsu is selling.

I played most of the game in the Bounty-adjacent armour that pays you for not missing. That is not a build guide. That is an admission that the sword is the feature and everything else is furniture.

## Ezo, not a checklist (until it is)

The island is why the score stays in the high eights. Yotei Grasslands will feel like Tsushima's greatest hits. Ishikari Plains go red and ashen and suddenly the sequel has a face. Weather is a character. The mountain is a compass you do not need a HUD for.

Progression is still pilgrimage: shrines for skills, hot springs for health, bamboo for spirit, a wolf who treats you like a colleague rather than a pet. The wolf dens are the one new activity I would keep if I were editing the design doc. Hunting with an animal that has its own grievance is a better thematic rhyme than another fox that wants you to follow it to a charm.

The Guiding Wind is back. So are the golden birds. So is the Uncharted cliff. After twenty hours the diegetic navigation is still more graceful than a compass pip, and the side tales are still a list. You will know when you have seen the template. You will keep riding anyway, because the ride is the text.

Atsu's childhood house lets you hold the touchpad and drop into a memory. The PS5 SSD flex is real. The idea is better than the frequency. I used it when the plot dragged me home, then forgot it existed for six hours of bounties. That is the sequel in miniature: a beautiful trick, under-used, sitting next to a hundred things you have already done.

## The hunt that rhymes

Here is the deduction. Almost every Yotei Six chapter does this: arrive, listen, clash, watch them leave in a cutscene, lick the wound, clash again. Individually the duels slap. As a season of television it is a writers' room that found one outline and photocopied it.

Atsu's companions — Oyuki especially — are the reason the middle does not flatten completely. They argue with the sash. The game is smarter when it lets someone tell Atsu she is boring. It is dumber when the next region resets the same chase.

I am not asking for a twist every forty minutes. I am asking the structure to stop announcing the interval.

## Consensus, credited

I scored this from my own save. Other desks are a temperature check, not a source text.

According to [IGN's Michael Higham](https://www.ign.com/articles/ghost-of-yotei-review), Yotei is **8/10** — "a great distillation of the samurai fantasy" that "may not be revolutionizing open world games." That is the sentence I kept circling. [Press Start Australia](https://press-start.com.au/reviews/ps5-reviews/2025/09/25/ghost-of-yotei-review/) landed at **8.5/10** and called it "another worthwhile trip to a lawless Japan" that does not quite catch Tsushima's first-play shock. [WellPlayed's Adam Ryan](https://www.well-played.com.au/ghost-of-yotei-review/) went **9.5/10** and wrote that "Atsu’s personal, honest story of revenge and self-reflection" makes it "an unforgettable masterpiece."

I am closer to Press Start than to WellPlayed. Ryan is righter than I am about the performances. I am colder on the photocopied hunts.

[Digitally Downloaded's Matt](https://www.digitallydownloaded.net/2025/10/review-ghost-of-yotei-sony-playstation-5.html) filed a different argument entirely — headline "More Quintin Tarantino than Akira Kurosawa" — about how thinly Ezo's Ainu presence sits inside a story of Japanese factions fighting over a land that was not empty. I am not restating that piece. I am noting it is the most useful Australian disagreement in the file, and that our 8.6 is a play-feel score, not a history grade. If that conversation matters to you, read Matt's piece before you spend first-party money.

## Australia: PS5, Pro, Plus, the till

This is a **PS5 exclusive**. There is no PC port to wait for and no Game Pass fantasy to hide in. If your living room is Xbox or a Steam Deck, this review is a window, not a shopping list.

On a base PS5 I stayed in Performance. The Pro's Ray Tracing Pro mode is the flex you show people who do not play games; it is also the mode I used when I wanted the mountain to look like a lie. Kurosawa Mode is still the black-and-white postcard. Miike Mode is the gore toggle. Watanabe Mode is lo-fi for the post-game ride. None of them change the score. They change the screenshot folder.

Australian pricing moves. The pattern does not: **PSN AU** is instant and full sticker; **EB Games** and **JB Hi-Fi** are the physical hunt and the later preowned copy. First-party Sony money in this country is not a casual tap. If you already bounce off open-world shrines, wait for a Plus Extra/Premium window rather than pretending a sale is a personality.

For the rest of the 2026 cabinet, Yotei belongs on [Best PS5 games 2026](/best-ps5-games-2026/) next to [Death Stranding 2](/death-stranding-2-review/) — two first-party walks that refuse to be in a hurry.

## Verdict

**8.6/10**. Atsu is the sequel Tsushima earned. Ezo is the reason the machine stays in the cabinet. The hunt is the reason I am not writing a 9. Buy it if the sword still lives in your thumbs. Skip it if you wanted a new open-world sentence and not a sharper version of the last one.
