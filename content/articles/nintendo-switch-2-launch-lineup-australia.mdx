Nintendo's next handheld is no longer a rumour spreadsheet. Australian retailers have the hardware, the docking story is clearer, and the launch window is less "maybe" than "how many hours can you take off work."

## The local picture

Pricing remains the conversation in group chats. Import taxes and the usual AU premium mean the sticker is not Tokyo's sticker. Major chains staggered pre-order waves; independent stores in Melbourne and Sydney reported cleaner allocations than the first Switch weekend — faint praise, but progress.

The software slate is a mix of showpiece first-party and the ports everyone expected. Mario is present. A new 3D platformer is the midnight-launch magnet. Third parties are treating the machine as a serious SKU rather than a charming afterthought, which is the real hardware story.

## What to actually buy week one

If you only pick up one game with the console, pick the one you will play in handheld on the tram. That has always been Nintendo's advantage. The living-room dock is nicer this generation; the identity is still the bag.

Accessories are already a second economy. Third-party docks will proliferate. Official Joy-Con revisions fix the problems you remember and introduce at least one new way to lose a shoulder button under the couch.

## The hangover to watch

Online infrastructure and Game Chat-style social features need to survive contact with Australian latency. We will report after the first firmware that is not a day-one apology. For now: the lineup is real, the stock is finite, and the "wait for a bundle" argument is weaker than it was in 2017.
