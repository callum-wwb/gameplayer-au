*Legacy of the Void* had an impossible job: close the *StarCraft II* story, satisfy the tournament scene, and justify a third box in a genre the mass market had already abandoned. It did the first two so well that the third stopped mattering. The campaign is still the gold standard for authored RTS missions. The ladder is still a language.

## Campaign as set-piece machine

Kerrigan's arc had already gone operatic. *LotV* brings it home with Protoss ritual and a mission list that keeps inventing toys. One level hands you a new mechanic and then asks you to break it. That is Blizzard campaign craft at its most confident — closer to a puzzle box than a skirmish.

Co-op commanders later became the accidental forever-mode: weekly mutations, hero kits, and a social RTS that does not require a 200 APM apology. If you only play *StarCraft II* on a Sunday night with one friend, that is a complete game.

## The ladder does not expire

LotV's multiplayer tweaks — starting workers, mineral changes, the feel of a faster early game — kept the sport watchable. Korean and international scenes treated the game as infrastructure. Even as Blizzard's attention wandered, the mechanics remained honest: input, information, and the terror of a well-placed widow mine.

## Verdict

**9.4/10**. The trilogy's best campaign, and the expansion that locked *StarCraft II* in as a classic rather than a product cycle. If you have only ever watched GSL highlights, play the missions. If you have only ever played the missions, try one co-op commander. The genre has not replaced this.
