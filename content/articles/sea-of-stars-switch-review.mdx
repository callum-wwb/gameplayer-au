Pixel-art RPGs have a nostalgia trap. *Sea of Stars* mostly avoids it by caring about turn timing, party identity, and a world that has jokes as well as crystals. The Sabotage team already proved they could do vibes with *The Messenger*. This is the fuller sentence.

## Combos, not just menus

Timed hits are back and they matter. Combos ask you to think about turn order instead of mashing the recommended spell. Bosses are encounters, not sponges. The soundtrack does the heavy lifting when the plot goes full prophecy — which it does, because of course it does.

Switch is the natural home. Handheld mode is how this art should be seen. Performance held in our playthrough; the occasional load is a breath, not a brick.

## The soft spots

Pacing dips in the back half. Some towns exist to be pretty. If you want systems as deep as *Baldur's Gate 3*, you are in the wrong decade on purpose.

## Verdict

**8.4/10**. Play it when you want an adventure that believes in villages, themes, and a battle theme you will hum in the kitchen.
