*Mario Kart World* is the first Kart that feels like Nintendo pointed the camera at a continent and said: drive there. The connected world is not a gimmick overlay. It is the product. That ambition is why this review exists, and why the score is not a 10.

I played docked and handheld on Switch 2, local four-player until the room got loud, and enough online to know the rubber-band still has opinions.

## The world, and why it matters

Courses are places you can leave. Intermissions are roads. Free roam is the Saturday morning mode: wander, hunt panels, take a stupid jump because the geography allowed it. Nintendo has been inching toward this since Tour's map fantasies. World actually ships the fantasy.

The cost is focus. A classic eight-race Grand Prix still sings, but the new knockout structure and the open map steal some of the old "this cup is a perfect object" feeling. I miss that object. I do not want to go back.

## Racing feel

Karting is still Mario Kart: items are chaos theology, shortcuts are homework, and blue shells remain a diplomatic incident. The new kit — more characters, more vehicles, more ways to be slightly extra — is generous to the point of noise. In a living room that is a feature. In Time Trials it can feel like the garage ate the sport.

Track design in the connected world is clever more often than it is iconic. A handful of routes I will remember for years. A handful I will remember as "the bit between two better bits."

## Multiplayer is the review

This is a party game that finally has a party map. Split-screen still asks the hardware to behave. Online still asks your NBN node to behave. When both do, World is the most *social* Kart Nintendo has shipped — not because the items got funnier, because the space between races stopped being a menu.

## Consensus, credited

According to [IGN's Logan Plant](https://www.ign.com/articles/mario-kart-world-review), World is "a massive, messy, and magical new beginning," scored **8/10**. I am a notch higher: the mess is real, the magic is the product, and a Switch 2 without this cartridge feels unfinished.

From Australia, [Digitally Downloaded's Matt S.](https://www.digitallydownloaded.net/2025/06/review-mario-kart-world-nintendo-switch-2.html) reviewed World as a Switch 2 living-room event, not a museum piece. That is the honest local frame. We are not copying his verdict. We are agreeing that the couch is the point.

## Australia: price and should you?

Nintendo's AU sticker on first-party launches is not subtle. Shop around: physical at EB / JB often beats a tired eShop moment, and a used copy will exist faster than Nintendo would like. If your household is still on Switch 1, **do not** treat this review as a reason to panic-buy a console. 8 Deluxe is not dead.

If you already have the Switch 2, this is the game you put in the dock when people come over. That is enough.

**Score: 8.8 / 10.**
