A "best consoles" list is a personality test. We scored on library, hardware ambition, and how completely the machine rewired the hobby. Handhelds would be a different article. These are the boxes under the TV.

## 5. Xbox 360

Online as infrastructure, not an experiment. A generation of multiplayer habits, for better and worse, starts here. The Red Ring is part of the folklore; so is the idea that a console could be a media centre without apology.

## 4. Super Nintendo

Mode 7, first-party peak, and the cartridge as a design constraint that produced miracles. If you only ever play Nintendo, you can still defend this as number one. We have it at four because the machines above it changed the business, not just the art.

## 3. PlayStation 2

The museum that sold 160 million tickets. DVD as trojan horse, a third-party flood, and the last time "a console" meant every genre at once. The PS2 did not invent cool. It industrialised it.

## 2. Nintendo Switch

The hybrid that made form factor the feature. A first-party run so strong it made hardware shortages feel like a personality. We docked it at two because the online story never matched the games — and because the machine above it still owns the living-room myth.

## 1. PlayStation (the original)

3D as a mass product, CDs as a business model, and a tone shift the industry never reversed. Sony did not just win a generation. It changed who got to make games for the living room.
