Say "gamer" in a capital-city pub and people still picture a TV. Check the revenue charts and the picture is a phone. That gap is the most boring argument in games writing and we keep having it because our desks are still arranged around living rooms.

## The audience already moved

Mobile is not a genre. It is a distribution system that happens to include *Genshin*, football managers, and a match-3 that funds a studio's art team. Treating it as a lesser platform is like treating paperbacks as lesser novels. Some of it is junk. So is a lot of the console store.

What changed is quality control at the top. Premium mobile can be excellent. Free-to-play can be extractive. Those are design choices, not destiny. A critic's job is to say which is which, not to pretend the App Store is a carnival we visit for a feature.

## What Australian coverage owes readers

Local payment rules, loot-box law, and the fact that a "free" game can be the most expensive hobby in a share house. If we only review boxed releases, we miss the games people actually argue about at work.

GamePlayer's Mobile section exists because the 2020 archive already knew this — *Heroes of Loot* sat next to console reviews without a permission slip. We are not discovering a trend. We are refusing to unlearn it.
