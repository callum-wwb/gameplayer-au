*Elden Ring* is the rare blockbuster that still lets you get lost on purpose. Miyazaki and Martin's collaboration is less a lore dump than a weather system: you feel the history before you can name it. The first ride into Limgrave remains one of the great opening hours in the medium.

## Freedom with teeth

Souls combat is intact — stamina, poise, the religion of the roll — but the map changes the psychology. You can leave. You can see a castle, decide you are under-levelled, and come back three nights later as a different build. That is not a checklist; it is a relationship with a landscape.

The Legacy Dungeons are the thesis. Stormveil, Leyndell, Farum Azula: vertical, cruel, and authored in a way most open worlds never attempt. When the game opens up, it never dilutes those spaces. It just gives you more of them.

## The scars

PC performance at launch was a mess we have not fully forgiven. Some late-game runbacks still feel like a dare. PvP remains a sideshow. None of that moves the score the way the first view of Liurnia does.
