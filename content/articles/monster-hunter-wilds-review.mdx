Capcom did not forget how to make a hunt feel expensive. *Monster Hunter Wilds* still turns a 15-minute fight into a short film about positioning, sharpness, and the exact second you should have sheathed. What it forgot, at launch, was how to make you *afraid* of the thing you are filming.

I hunted on PS5 and PC, solo with Support Hunters, then in a three-human SOS that spent more time laughing at a cart than carting. The weapons still sing. The Forbidden Lands look like a nature documentary that wants you dead. The campaign let me walk out the door without learning a single defensive jewel.

## The hunt, sanded

Quality-of-life is the star. You stay in the field. Meals last on a timer instead of a ritual. The Seikret is the Wirebug's calmer cousin: less circus, more "get me to the next turf war." Weather actually changes a locale's face — sandstorm, plenty, downpour — even when it does not always change the *math*.

Focus Mode and Wounds give every weapon a shared mini-game. When it clicks, you are a surgeon. When the monster is already melting, you are a surgeon performing on a training dummy. That is the review in one wound break.

New monsters have swagger. Returning ones have better lighting. Arkveld is a headliner that understands spectacle. I still wanted one launch elder that sent me back to camp to think.

## Story, then steam

The cast is likable in a way Monster Hunter stories rarely bother with. Alma, Gemma, Erik — they have pulses. The plot is still "uncharted ecosystem, please hit it," and I am fine with that. I am less fine with a credits roll that arrives before a single hunt has asked me to rebuild a set.

Post-game is where Wilds starts speaking fluent Monster Hunter. Title updates matter more than usual because the launch endgame is a foyer. If you only review the foyer, you undersell the house Capcom intends to build. If you only review the roadmap, you are reviewing a press release. I scored the game I played, then noted the scaffolding.

PC launch performance was a known mess; later patches walked it back. Play on console if you want the lower-stress install. Play on PC if you want the sharper hunt and you are willing to keep Steam updated like a ritual.

## Consensus, credited

According to [IGN's Tom Marks](https://www.ign.com/articles/monster-hunter-wilds-review), Wilds is "smoothing out the already excellent process" of the hunt, scored **8/10**, while calling out how little it asked him to think about equipment. That is uncomfortably close to my notebook. I am a tenth higher on combat feel, a tenth lower on how fast the grind evaporated, and we land in the same band.

## Australia: should you buy it?

Full live-service-adjacent price, but this is still a complete Capcom hunt, not a battle pass wearing a wyvern. Buy it if you have two friends and a Sunday. Wait for a sale if you are a World/Rise veteran who mainly shows up for the expansion wall. On AU storefronts, watch Steam and PSN — JB's physical bundle is occasionally the quiet winner.

If you have never hunted, this is the most polite door Capcom has ever installed. Walk through it. Just know the building gets more interesting after the credits, and after the patches.

**Score: 8.1 / 10.**
