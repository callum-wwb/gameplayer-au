Mobile dungeon crawlers usually make one of two mistakes: they pretend they are a PC ARPG and drown you in menus, or they are so thin they evaporate after the tutorial. *Heroes of Loot* sits in the useful middle. It is Gauntlet by way of a peak-hour train — four classes, randomised floors, gold everywhere, and a death that does not feel like a lecture.

## Pocket-sized Gauntlet

You pick a hero, you walk into a dungeon, you shoot the things that move. The camera is top-down, the rooms are chunky, and the loot is frequent enough that you always feel one chest away from a better staff. The art is bright without being childish, which matters on a phone screen in sunlight.

The real design win is session length. A floor takes a few minutes. A failed run does not confiscate your afternoon. That is rarer than it should be in a store full of energy timers.

## Where it levels off

There is not a lot of mechanical surprise after the first hour. Enemy types repeat, bosses telegraph generously, and the meta-progression is more of a polite nudge than a second game. If you are coming from *Hades* or *Dead Cells*, you will feel the ceiling.

Controls are the other fork. Virtual sticks are fine until a room fills with projectiles. A Bluetooth controller helps; thumbs-only play is acceptable, not excellent.

## Verdict

*Heroes of Loot* is an honest mobile crawler. It does not reinvent the dungeon. It just lets you visit one between stops. **7.2/10** — recommended for short sessions, not for your next obsession.
